2023 had a lot of ups and downs. I'm writing this to look back on what happened, what I learned, and what I want from next year. Think of it as a chat with myself that you get to read.

Let's roll.

### 1. Take the initiative

The biggest lesson this year was to take the first step instead of waiting for someone else to.

In my final year of college I had to write a thesis. A lot of students struggled with their professors. They couldn't find a time to meet, or their drafts sat for weeks without corrections. I tried a different approach. I reached out to my professor myself, kept him updated on my progress, and suggested we meet at his coffee shop to go through my drafts. I also made a group chat for everyone he supervised, so my friends could check his schedule without chasing him. It made things easier for all of us, and it made me stand out to him.

Reaching out first also got me job opportunities. I asked a senior engineer I knew whether there was a role that fit me, and asked for a referral. I even messaged a company's CEO directly. LinkedIn, X, and email make this easy now. It helps a lot to have something to show, like projects or a decent profile, so people have a reason to trust you.

I used to be scared of reaching out. I always assumed the other person would be annoyed. Honestly, it doesn't matter much as long as you're polite. The worst answer you can get is no, so go ask.

### 2. Always follow up

I lost a lot of opportunities because I didn't follow up. Whether you're applying for a job or pitching a client, focus on what you can do for them and the problems they have, without expecting something back right away.

The other person needs to see that you're good at the things they struggle with. Following up is how you show it. Send a short message with something you've built or done since you last talked. Do it more than once, with some time in between. You'll get ignored and rejected along the way. Over time it stops hurting, and you start thinking, "Okay, next."

### 3. Handling rejection

This one is close to the first two, but from a different angle. Rejection sucks, and everyone hates it.

I've been rejected so many times that I started questioning my own worth. Every rejection felt like proof that I wasn't good enough for anything, and getting back up was hard.

Now I try to see rejection as practice instead of a verdict on me. Often the reason has nothing to do with you. The timing was wrong, the budget disappeared, or they already had someone in mind.

It also keeps me humble. It reminds me to be grateful for what I already have and what I've learned so far.

### 4. Don't depend on your routine

I'm obsessed with self-development, so I built a routine. Wake up, grab a coffee, work out, and so on. The problem is that I started to need it. If I skipped my morning coffee, I couldn't get anything done and the whole day felt bad. The rest of the routine was the same. Missing one step threw off my whole day.

After a lot of bad days and a lot of procrastination, I realized that leaning on a routine this hard was making me weaker. Needing coffee to work is like telling the world how it has to be before you'll do anything, and you don't control the world.

Think about how it goes. At first, coffee helps you feel better and get things done. Over time, you need it just to feel normal. The boost is gone, and now it's a requirement.

People a hundred years ago got plenty done without any of this.

I still have routines, and I'm planning more for next year. The difference is that I don't want my work to depend on them. If the coffee isn't there, the work still gets done. That's easier said than done, but it makes me more resilient.

### 5. You'll never feel ready

This applies to almost everything. It's good to learn the basics before you start, but you don't need to be perfect. Waiting is tempting, and it usually turns into procrastination. Whether it's starting something new or sharing your work, there's always a voice saying you need more skills first. But when is it ever enough?

The truth is you'll never feel completely ready. After reading and watching a lot about this, my conclusion is that the best time to start is while you're still unsure.

### 6. Less social media, more peace of mind

I've cut way back on social media. I mostly open it for important moments in the lives of my close friends or my girlfriend, to post or send a message so they know I see them. I don't think it's realistic to quit completely, since so much happens online now. But I do think you need limits so it doesn't take over.

I've always preferred talking face to face over chatting online. I still scroll mindlessly sometimes, especially on hard days. When I notice it, I log out or hide the apps on days when I need zero distractions.

### 7. A few good friends are enough

I've lost touch with many friends along the way. Not because we fought, but because everyone has their own life and goes in their own direction. Sometimes I just don't click with someone, because of their attitude, the environment, or maybe because of me. I've learned to be okay with that, even though it was hard.

I have a small group of close friends who push me to keep going. Some of them are better than me at certain things, and they inspire and support me. That's enough.

### 8. Relationships and love

I learned a lot about relationships this year, especially with the people closest to me. I understand now why looks don't matter much when you have someone who supports you no matter what, and I'm really thankful for that. What matters is talking openly. Whatever comes up, we trust each other, we don't set strict rules, and we support each other. I give advice when she needs it, and I agree with her when it just feels right, even if she's technically wrong. Haha.

Like in any relationship, people need to feel valued and respected. That's the main thing I learned.

### 9. Health is your superpower

I got sick too often this year. It got bad enough that I developed GERD from delaying meals all the time, and it felt terrible. For some reason it always hit right before something important, whether it was a family reunion or just meeting friends at a coffee shop. I've also seen relatives with plenty of money who spend every month dealing with health problems and hospital check-ups.

Life is a marathon. In the end, I just want a decent life. I want my parents to be able to retire, I want to help my friends and the people I love, and I want to pay my bills. None of that happens if my health falls apart. Even if it takes 10 years, as long as I stay healthy I can keep working toward it.

### 10. Learn, practice, repeat

Never stop learning. The pace of trends in software engineering used to burn me out. Now I think what you need is solid fundamentals, patience, and a strong work ethic. Most tools and frameworks share the same ideas, so what you understand in one carries over to the next. My approach is to pick one thing, like React for web development, while making sure I understand the JavaScript, HTML, and CSS underneath. Then build a project. It doesn't need to be fancy, just enough to make the knowledge stick.

If you keep a steady pace and stay patient, you'll at least be able to handle the basics when an opportunity shows up, and grow from there. I often feel pressure to master something in one go, which is impossible, at least for me.

Everything is built for instant gratification now. That makes patience and the habit of learning rare, even for me. So I'm going to keep learning until it's just part of who I am.

That's the list. I could keep going, but these are the ones that stuck.

### Eight things I'm going to do in 2024

#### 1. Go to the gym regularly

It keeps me healthy, and looking good is a bonus. I've noticed a lot of people in great shape who just wear simple outfits and still look great. I want that. Haha.

#### 2. Focus on learning more, not earning more

I'm still young, and a lot will happen. What I need now is experience, and if I chase money too early I'll stop learning. I want to pick up every skill I can along the way, not only software engineering.

#### 3. Practice my English writing and speaking

I need this, because right now I still rely on ChatGPT to help write this blog. Lol. I also want to speak English fluently so I can take more opportunities. I'm starting with Duolingo, reading and writing English blogs, and watching English videos.

#### 4. Make YouTube videos about what I know

Almost everything I've learned came from videos, mostly on YouTube. I'd like to give something back by sharing what I know.

#### 5. Write a blog post at least once a month

I want a place to think out loud, and it doubles as English practice.

#### 6. Be a better son, brother, partner, and friend

I've made a lot of mistakes and have probably upset people without knowing it. I want to do better for them, and also learn to respect myself.

#### 7. Invest in stocks

I want to learn how to make my money work for me. The rules are to invest only a set budget, and to understand a company before buying its stock.

#### 8. Build a proper portfolio and blog

With a proper tech stack and my own domain. I want people to be able to subscribe and comment, so I can get feedback on my writing.

### Closing

This post is mostly for me, so I can look back on it next year and laugh. It took longer to write than I expected.
